# Medieval and Later: Rebis, Rose, and Expanded Family Trees

## Separate developments

1. **Holy Kinship:** Medieval stories expand Jesus's family through Anne's
   relatives and, in some versions, three daughters named Mary. See
   [Holy Cross's account](https://college.holycross.edu/projects/kempe/devotion/holy_kin/holykin.html).
   It does not name Anne's mother in that account.
2. **Emerentia:** Some late-medieval sources name her as Anne's mother and
   describe a prophetic root-and-tree vision. A
   [1515–1530 sculpture](https://www.christianiconography.info/december2001/emerantia.html)
   depicts her with Anne, Mary, and Jesus.
3. **Rebis and the chemical wedding:** A [15th-century alchemical miniature](https://openn.library.upenn.edu/Data/0023/html/lewis_e_m_028_026-027.html)
   depicts a crowned unified androgyne or Rebis. [Princeton's explanation](https://dpul.princeton.edu/alchemy/feature/the-chemical-wedding)
   discusses sun-and-moon union in a Ripley Scroll. The object and
   accompanying symbols need to be read in their own alchemical contexts.
4. **Emorun:** The nineteenth-century [Emmerich/Brentano visionary account](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/emmerich/lifemary.v.html)
   names Moruni or Emorun and depicts a rose tree with three branches. Its
   family tree differs from the Emerentia-as-Anne's-mother version.

These traditions overlap in the investigation's imagery but do not
automatically document one continuous secret line. Emorun's vision does
not identify her with Barbelo or with the Rebis.
