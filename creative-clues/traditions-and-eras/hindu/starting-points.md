# Hindu Traditions: Ultimate Reality, Avatars, and the Nine Grahas

The chat asks about Hindu gods and compares them with other divine names.
Hindu traditions are living, diverse traditions, not a single fixed
genealogical chart. These are starting points for specific questions.

| Clue | Starting evidence | Important distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brahman | The [Upanishadic concept of brahman](https://www.britannica.com/topic/brahman-Hindu-concept) concerns ultimate reality; Vedanta schools interpret its relation to the world and personal deity differently. | Brahman is not automatically equivalent to the Monad in another text. |
| Brahma | [Brahma](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Brahma-Hindu-god) is a creator deity. | Brahma, brahman, and the Buddhist Brahma Sahampati are not interchangeable just because their names resemble one another. |
| Vishnu's forms | The [Dashavatara](https://www.britannica.com/topic/dashavatara) groups ten principal avatars of Vishnu in a tradition with varying lists. | Ten incarnations are not ten cosmic resets or evidence that unrelated historical people are one being. |
| Yugas | One [cosmological scheme](https://www.britannica.com/topic/yuga) describes four yugas in a repeating larger cycle. | A religious account of cosmic time is not a verified chronology of human DNA across universes. |
| Navagraha | [Navagraha](https://www.britannica.com/topic/navagraha) means nine grahas: Sun, Moon, five visible planets, and Rahu and Ketu. | Rahu and Ketu correspond to the lunar nodes, not physical planets. A nine-graha system is not the chat's universal seven-person lineage chart. |

Shani is associated with Saturn in Indian astrology; that shared planetary
reference does not make Shani, Cronus, and a ruler from the *Apocryphon of
John* the same individual. See the [planetary comparison](../greek-olympian/planets-weekdays-and-archons.md).
