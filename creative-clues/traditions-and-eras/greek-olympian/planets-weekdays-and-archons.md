# Planets, Weekdays, and the Seven Rulers

**Question:** Which connections in the chat's planet chart are historically
traceable, and which are later comparisons?

## The weekday trail

The classical seven are the Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, and
Saturn. The Sun and Moon were counted among the wandering heavenly bodies in
this older scheme; they are not planets in modern astronomy. The
[Viking Ship Museum](https://www.vikingeskibsmuseet.dk/en/professions/education/viking-age-people/the-names-of-the-weekdays)
explains the Roman and Germanic weekday connections; the English forms below
use Old English names, with their familiar Norse counterparts in parentheses.

| English day | Roman planetary day | English name or counterpart |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sunday | Sun / Sol | Sun |
| Monday | Moon / Luna | Moon |
| Tuesday | Mars | Tiw (Tyr) |
| Wednesday | Mercury | Woden (Odin) |
| Thursday | Jupiter | Thunor (Thor) |
| Friday | Venus | Frig (Frigg) |
| Saturday | Saturn | Saturn |

Friday's Old English name is connected to Frig, corresponding to Norse
Frigg; the Latin weekday is Venus's day. Frigg and Freyja are distinct
figures in surviving Norse myths, although theories about their earlier
relationship are debated. These weekday correspondences show how cultures
adapted names, not that the gods were literally the same person or members
of one family.

## The *Apocryphon* is not the chat's planet chart

The [*Apocryphon of John*, translated by Frederik Wisse](https://earlychristianwritings.com/text/apocryphonjohn.html),
lists seven powers and calls the list the sevenness of the week. In order,
their names and faces are:

1. Athoth — sheep.
2. Eloaiou — donkey.
3. Astaphaios — hyena.
4. Yao — seven-headed serpent.
5. Sabaoth — dragon.
6. Adonin — monkey.
7. Sabbede — shining fire-face.

The same narrative also lists **twelve authorities**, with seven kings over
the heavens and five over the abyss; it is important not to collapse that
list into the seven faces. The translation separately calls a figure named
Cain the sun among the twelve. It does not provide the chat's fixed
planet-by-planet table for the seven faces: for example, the chat labels
Sabaoth as monkey-faced, but this list gives Sabaoth the dragon face and
Adonin the monkey face.

Other ancient works may arrange rulers or heavenly spheres differently;
their mappings must be checked in their own passages. Seven weekdays, seven
Pleiades, and seven rulers are not automatically the same seven beings.
See [Seven brides, stars, lamps, and thunder](../../categories/sevens-brides-and-stars.md).

## Planetary science is a separate question

The [Grand Tack model](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/proceedings-of-the-international-astronomical-union/article/grand-tack-model-a-critical-review/64A53C99E2A2078879C0F16C808FF447)
proposes early inward and outward migration of Jupiter, linked to Saturn,
as one explanation for the structure of the inner Solar System. It is a
scientific model with uncertainties, not proof that Earth would otherwise
have been destroyed. Names of celestial bodies can preserve mythological
associations without implying that mythical beings are physically trapped
in them.
