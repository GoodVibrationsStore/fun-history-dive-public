# Levantine and Israelite: El, Athirat, and the Most High

## Starting evidence

Ugaritic texts from the Late Bronze Age preserve stories featuring El,
Athirat, Baal, and other divine figures. For editions, see [*The Ugaritic
Baal Cycle*](https://brill.com/abstract/title/1061) and [Wiggins's study of
Athirat](https://era.ed.ac.uk/items/1a86132d-3a63-4458-9592-d46921b5a8d5).
These texts do not provide a biological family tree connecting the deities
to Jesus or John.

The Hebrew Bible uses *El Elyon* (“God Most High”) in
[Genesis 14:18–22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis%2014%3A18-22&version=NRSVUE)
and calls YHWH the Most High in
[Psalm 83:18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%2083%3A18&version=NRSVUE).
[Proverbs 8:22–31](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs%208%3A22-31&version=NRSVUE)
personifies Wisdom beside God in creation; it does not call her a wife.

## Questions, not conclusions

- **El and YHWH:** Some scholars reconstruct a convergence of traditions;
  other readings emphasize their identification. No passage alone proves a
  literal divine overthrow.
- **YHWH and “his asherah”:** Scholars dispute whether the inscriptional
  phrase names a goddess or a cultic object. See the fuller
  [Most High comparison](../../categories/most-high-comparison.md).
- **Wisdom and Sophia:** Greek *sophia* means wisdom, but the Wisdom of
  Proverbs and Sophia in different early Christian myths have different
  textual roles. A shared word is a comparison clue, not a complete identity.
