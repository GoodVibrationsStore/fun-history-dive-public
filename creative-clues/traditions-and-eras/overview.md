# Tracing the Threads Across Traditions and Eras

These folders make the investigation easier to navigate. They are organized
by **tradition and period**, not as a claim that one culture replaced
another: Egyptian, Mesopotamian, Levantine, and Greek traditions overlapped
for centuries.

## What “tracing the line” can mean

1. **A biological line:** Check each mother–child connection and seek
   independent evidence. The named historical line does not extend into
   divine genealogies just because the stories share a theme.
2. **A textual line:** Show a demonstrable quotation, translation, retelling,
   or manuscript relationship.
3. **A cultural connection:** Establish historical contact and evaluate
   scholarly arguments for influence. Similarity is a starting point, not
   proof of direct borrowing or one deity's identity with another.
4. **A creative line:** Connect symbols across traditions deliberately,
   while labeling the joins as a story model.

The [maternal-line investigation](../categories/maternal-line-and-emerentia.md)
gives separate candidate trees and explains where the documented links stop.
The [light/dark and Rebis grid](../light-dark-rebis/pairings-and-maternal-source.md)
compares proposed divine pairings without presenting them as proven names
for the same beings.

## A working timeline of evidence—not the age of gods

- **Third millennium BCE:** An/Anu is securely attested in Mesopotamian
  evidence; Egyptian written religious traditions also have deep roots.
- **Late Bronze Age:** Ugaritic tablets preserve stories about El, Athirat,
  Baal, and other gods. Other Egyptian and Mesopotamian traditions remain
  active.
- **First millennium BCE:** Hebrew Bible texts and Greek literary
  traditions develop over different periods. Hesiod's *Theogony* is
  commonly placed around the late eighth or early seventh century BCE.
- **First centuries CE:** Early Christian texts and diverse later
  interpretations of biblical stories circulate.
- **Medieval to nineteenth century:** Alchemical images, expanded holy-family
  genealogies, and Emmerich's visions belong to distinct later contexts.

These ranges are research signposts, not exact first appearances of every
figure. A text's composition, its surviving manuscript, and the events it
describes may belong to different times. An older written attestation cannot
prove which divine being is “really” oldest.

## Cross-cultural bridges to test

| Lead | What can be investigated | What remains unproved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Divine succession | Scholars compare Hesiod's succession story with Near Eastern traditions; see [Scully's study](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2016/2016.05.25). | Similar plots do not establish that the gods are the same individuals or belong to one biological line. |
| El and YHWH | Hebrew passages use El language and identify YHWH with titles such as Most High in some contexts; scholars debate earlier development. | A single deliberate takeover or the identity of every divine name. |
| Biblical Wisdom and later Sophia | Wisdom is personified in Proverbs; later works use Greek *sophia* in different stories. | That all references depict the same being or an ancestor of Mary. |
| Maternal branch and rose | Later holy-family trees and the Emorun vision provide distinct traditions. | A documented mtDNA connection to ancient deities or survival across cosmic resets. |
| Sun–moon union and Rebis | Medieval alchemical art joins opposing principles, sometimes as a chemical wedding. | That ancient divine pairs literally merged into a particular person. |

**Research status:** These are starting notes, not an exhaustive history or
a full review of the shared chat. Add a specific source and a dated witness
for each proposed link before moving it from “comparison” to “documented.”
