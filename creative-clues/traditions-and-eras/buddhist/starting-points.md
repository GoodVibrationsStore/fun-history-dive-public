# Buddhist Traditions: Devas, Awakening, and Compassion

The chat asks about Buddhist gods. Buddhist traditions differ by region,
school, and text; devas, buddhas, and bodhisattvas have different roles and
should not be flattened into one pantheon.

| Clue | Starting evidence | Important distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brahma Sahampati | In the [Ayacana Sutta, SN 6.1](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN6_1.html), Brahma Sahampati asks the newly awakened Buddha to teach. | The name resembles Hindu Brahma and brahman, but the roles and textual contexts must be examined separately. |
| Bodhisattvas | A [bodhisattva](https://www.britannica.com/topic/bodhisattva) is associated with the path to buddhahood; Mahayana traditions emphasize helping other beings. | It is too broad to say that all bodhisattvas are already fully enlightened or follow one identical account of postponement. |
| Tara | [Tara](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Tara-Buddhist-goddess) is a compassionate, protective figure with multiple forms, including Green and White Tara, in Buddhist traditions. | Shared feminine or protective imagery does not establish that she is Sophia, Mary, or an ancestor of a human family. |
| Lotus image | In SN 6.1, the Buddha compares beings' different capacities to lotuses at different levels in water. | This is a useful teaching image in that passage, not evidence that the lotus and Emorun's rose record the same family. |

The chat's description of “four primary classes” is a modern organizing
shortcut, not a demonstrated universal classification across Buddhism.
Likewise, comparing religious ideas about rebirth with a fictional reset
story does not prove that the traditions describe the same cycle.
