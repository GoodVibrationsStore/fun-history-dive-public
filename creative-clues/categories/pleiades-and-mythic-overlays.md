# Pleiades and Mythic Overlays

**Question:** Which parts of the seven-sisters comparison appear in ancient
sources, and which belong to the conversation's creative framework?

## What the sources say

In [Pseudo-Apollodorus, Library 3.10.1](https://www.theoi.com/Nymphe/NympheAlkyone.html),
the Pleiades are seven daughters of Atlas and Pleione. Alcyone is one of
them; the same passage associates her with Poseidon. Zeus is associated with
some of the sisters' children, not described there as their father.

The same source page reproduces a list attributed to Hesiod in which Alcyone
appears third; Apollodorus lists her first. A place in a list is not
necessarily a birth order. The chat's assertion that Alcyone was definitely
the **fourth-born** sister is not established by these sources.

Hyginus's *Astronomica* 2.21, also quoted on the source page, records a
tradition about seven Pleiades but only six visible stars. That is an ancient
explanation of the star cluster, not a way to identify people in a family.
Visibility also depends on observing conditions.

## Keep names and stories separate

Alcyone the Pleiad and Alcyone the wife of Ceyx appear in different family
stories. The latter is associated with the halcyon bird and calm seas; see
[Ovid, Metamorphoses 11](https://www.poetryintranslation.com/PITBR/Latin/Metamorph11.php).
Do not automatically transfer one figure's story to the other because they
share a name.

The chat's mapping of Sekhmet and seven sisters onto a sequence of cycles
can be a creative overlay. It is not, without a specific ancient source,
evidence that Egyptian and Greek traditions taught that same sequence or
that a person occupies a predestined position in it.

**Next step:** Compare named passages individually, recording the author and
the order actually given, before using any proposed cross-cultural match as
historical evidence.
