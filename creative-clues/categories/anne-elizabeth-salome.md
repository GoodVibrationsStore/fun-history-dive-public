# Anne, Elizabeth, Salome, and the Family-Tree Puzzle

The shared conversation proposes several maternal branches and asks whether
one was displaced by another. It also mixes biblical accounts, apocryphal
stories, later family legends, and creative ideas. Here are the layers we
can identify so far.

## What each source actually says

1. **Luke 1:** [Elizabeth is called Mary's relative](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%201%3A5-7%2C36%2C39-45&version=NRSVUE),
   but their exact degree of kinship is not specified. Elizabeth is said to
   descend from Aaron and her husband Zechariah to be a priest; Luke does not
   establish a specific Zadokite genealogy. The child leaps in Elizabeth's
   womb, but the passage does not describe a hereditary pact or genetic
   transfer.
2. **Protevangelium of James:** This second-century text names Mary's parents
   Joachim and Anna, describes Mary's birth after their childlessness, and
   tells a story about a woman named Salome at Jesus's birth. See the
   [scholarly overview](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/protevangelium-of-james/)
   and [translation, chapters 1–5 and 19–20](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0847.htm).
   It does not establish that its Salome is the Salome in Mark.
3. **Medieval Holy Kinship:** One later legend gives Anne three daughters named
   Mary and makes Elizabeth the daughter of Anne's sister Hismeria. See
   [College of the Holy Cross, Holy Kinship](https://college.holycross.edu/projects/kempe/devotion/holy_kin/holykin.html).
   That is a particular medieval family tree, not a genealogy supplied by
   Luke or the Protevangelium.

## Claims requiring stronger evidence

- The chat first treated Elizabeth as Anne's daughter, then offered other
  family trees. Luke does not say Elizabeth is Anne's daughter or name Anne.
- It asserted that a woman called the older Mary was displaced by Anne, and
  that this explains a stolen inheritance. No dated source or evidence of
  that event was provided; a repeated name does not prove a theft.
- It identified Sarah as Salome's hidden daughter and claimed that her role
  as attendant was a cover. These claims need specific early sources, not
  inference from later legends. See [Women and Provence](./women-and-provence.md).
- It treated the beloved disciple at the cross as John the Baptist.
  [John 19:25–27](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2019%3A25-27&version=NRSVUE)
  does not name that disciple; equating him with John the Baptist is not
  established by that passage.

## Inheritance is not identity

Maternal ancestry is a valid genealogical question. Ordinarily both sons and
daughters inherit mitochondrial DNA from their mother, while only daughters
pass it to their children. A proposed path through a father to his child is
therefore not ordinarily a continuous maternal mtDNA path. Shared mtDNA also
does not identify a particular ancient individual or establish spiritual
status. See [Britannica on mitochondrial inheritance](https://www.britannica.com/science/How-are-mitochondrial-DNA-disorders-passed-from-parent-to-child).

**Next step:** Draw a separate family tree for each identified source, marking
relationships as stated, inferred, or not specified. Do not join the trees
until there is evidence for each link.
