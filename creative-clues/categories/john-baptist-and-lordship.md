# John the Baptist and the "Lord" Question

## The working hypothesis

The proposed theory is that John the Baptist and Elizabeth's branch preserved
the true or "light" line, and that Mary, Jesus's mother, and her mother Anne
displaced or took a role or title belonging to John. This is a question to
investigate, not a conclusion established by the sources below. "Light line"
is the investigation's term; do not assume ancient sources used it for a
hereditary office.

The family-tree details and the separately attested later traditions are
mapped in [Anne, Elizabeth, and Salome](./anne-elizabeth-salome.md).

## Evidence board

| Source | Observation | What it can and cannot tell us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Mark 1:4–11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%201%3A4-11&version=NRSVUE) | John baptizes Jesus; the narrative also has John predict someone more powerful. | Both are in a Christian narrative. The baptism makes John's importance a real historical question, but does not establish a stolen title. |
| [Josephus, Antiquities 18.116–119](https://lexundria.com/j_aj/18.116-18.119/wst), written in the 90s CE | A non-Christian author describes John as influential, a teacher of virtue and baptism, killed by Herod. This passage does not mention Jesus. | Independent evidence of a substantial role for John, not evidence that he claimed to be the Messiah or that Mary or Anne displaced him. Josephus and Mark also describe the circumstances of his death differently. |
| [Luke 1:36–45](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%201%3A36-45&version=NRSVUE) | Elizabeth calls Mary the mother of “my Lord”; the unborn child leaps, which Elizabeth describes as joy. | Luke's account gives Jesus a prominent role. It does not describe an agreement, inheritance transfer, deception, or Anne's involvement. |
| [Luke 7:18–23](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%207%3A18-23&version=NRSVUE) | John sends disciples to ask Jesus whether he is the one to come. | A meaningful question about the relationship as Luke tells it; not an explicit claim that John himself was the rightful heir. |
| [John 1:6–8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%201%3A6-8&version=NRSVUE) | The Gospel portrays John as a witness to the light, not the light itself. | This is directly relevant to the light-line question, but is the author's theological framing, not independent evidence that a former identity was taken away. |
| [John 3:25–30](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%203%3A25-30&version=NRSVUE) | John's disciples report Jesus attracting followers; John is portrayed as the bridegroom's friend and says he must decrease. | Possible evidence for how this Gospel frames competing followings, not a neutral recording or proof of a historical succession dispute. |
| [Acts 19:1–7](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%2019%3A1-7&version=NRSVUE) | Acts depicts disciples familiar with John's baptism who then receive Christian baptism. | This is Acts' Christian portrayal; the passage does not give us the followers' independent account or prove they called John Lord. |
| [1 Corinthians 8:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Corinthians%208%3A6&version=NRSVUE) | Paul calls Jesus Lord in a letter generally dated to the 50s CE. | This shows the title was applied to Jesus well before the fourth-century Council of Nicaea. It does not establish what every follower of John believed earlier. |

**Lord is a title, not a known family office.** Greek *kyrios* can mean
lord, master, or sir according to context; it is also used in Greek biblical
traditions for God. Messiah/Christ, prophet, and priest are different
categories. Before asking whether a title was taken, identify which title,
who is claimed to have held it, and the earliest source that says so.

## A different living tradition

Mandaeans revere John in a living religious tradition that is distinct from
Christianity. Scholar James McGrath describes a passage in the
[Mandaean Book of John](https://www.patheos.com/blogs/religionprof/2011/11/the-baptism-of-jesus-by-john-in-the-mandaean-book-of-john.html)
in which Jesus comes to John for baptism; he discusses its polemical use of
Christian material. A
[critical text and translation](https://digitalcommons.butler.edu/facsch_papers/1065/)
by Häberl and McGrath is available for further study. Different traditions
about John are important evidence of different beliefs, but their existence
does not establish an unbroken first-century bloodline or a specific theft.
Dating individual traditions and surviving manuscripts requires further work.

The earlier chat's statement that church councils rewrote a rightful
succession, or that John was directly Zadokite, went beyond the evidence
identified there. Luke calls Elizabeth a descendant of Aaron and Zechariah a
priest, not a member of a documented Zadokite line. Anne is not named in Luke.

## The visitation and the “agreement”

In [Luke 1:39–45](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%201%3A39-45&version=NRSVUE),
the child leaps when Elizabeth hears Mary's greeting, and Elizabeth describes
the movement as joy. The passage does not say that Elizabeth kneels or that
the child makes a binding transfer of authority. The words about increasing
and decreasing belong to the adult Baptist in the separate scene in
[John 3:25–30](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%203%3A25-30&version=NRSVUE),
not to the unborn child in Luke. Neither scene establishes a biological pact.

The chat's assertion that councils rewrote these scenes to conceal John's
original hereditary office needs actual manuscript evidence. Different
portrayals and theological emphasis are valid subjects for comparison, but
they do not on their own establish an earlier text with the proposed office.

## Tests that could change the assessment

1. Find an independently datable source claiming John held a specific title
   later claimed for Jesus, and examine its date and context.
2. Locate an actual manuscript variant or early quotation that changes a
   relevant passage; compare witnesses rather than assuming a lost version.
3. Find a source connecting Mary or Anne to an identifiable historical act of
   displacement. Family-name resemblance alone would not demonstrate it.
4. Compare the possibility of rivalry or theological interpretation with the
   alternative that Christian authors sincerely regarded John as a forerunner.
