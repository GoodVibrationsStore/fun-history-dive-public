# Jesus's Maternal Line and Its Branches

## What this board traces

The question is not only “Who was Mary's mother?” but also: **Which aunts,
great-aunts, cousins, and other relatives are presented as coming from the
same maternal line?** The trees below follow the named women and their
children as far as the cited accounts say. They are separate hypotheses
from different sources, not one verified family tree.

For individual profiles and their children, see
[Maternal relatives by person](../maternal-relatives/README.md).

Arrows mean **mother → child**, not marriage. A woman ordinarily passes
mtDNA to all her children; a son ordinarily does not pass it on to his
children. Thus a maternal aunt can share someone's maternal line, but a
paternal aunt need not. Even on a continuous maternal branch, sequences
can differ through mutations or heteroplasmy. A named relationship in a
legend is not genetic evidence.

## 1. Earliest surviving textual starting points

```text
Unknown maternal ancestors
          ↓
Mary, mother of Jesus
          ↓
Jesus
```

- [Luke 1:5, 36, 39–45](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%201%3A5-7%2C36%2C39-45&version=NRSVUE)
  identifies Elizabeth, John the Baptist's mother, as Mary's relative, but
  does not name their common ancestor or say the relationship is through
  their mothers. Elizabeth's Aaronite ancestry does not itself establish an
  unbroken maternal path.
- [Mark 6:3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%206%3A3&version=NRSVUE)
  names Jesus's brothers and mentions sisters without naming them. Christian
  traditions differ over whether these are children of Mary, step-siblings,
  or other relatives. **If** a sister were Mary's biological daughter, she
  would ordinarily share Mary's mtDNA and could pass it to her children;
  Mark alone does not establish that branch.
- [John 19:25](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2019%3A25&version=NRSVUE)
  mentions Jesus's mother's sister near the cross, but the punctuation and
  identities are interpreted differently. It does not securely identify
  a biological maternal aunt or a continuing mtDNA branch.
- The second-century [*Protevangelium of James*, chapters 1–5](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0847.htm)
  adds **Anna** as Mary's mother. It does not name Anna's mother or sisters.
  This is a traditional claim, not an independently documented DNA pedigree.

**Stopping point:** The texts give no verified mtDNA sequence for Mary or
Jesus and do not supply a documented maternal tree into earlier eras.

## 2. Byzantine three-sister tradition

This is the version quoted in a [secondary source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobe_(sister_of_Saint_Anne)),
attributed there to later Byzantine writers. Its Greek source and transmission
still need direct checking. The name of the sisters' mother here is **Mary,
wife of Matthan**, not Emerentia.

```text
Mary, wife of Matthan
├── elder Mary ──→ Salome, described as a midwife
├── Sobe       ──→ Elizabeth ──→ John the Baptist
└── Anne       ──→ Mary, mother of Jesus ──→ Jesus
```

In this account, the elder Mary and Sobe are the Virgin Mary's aunts;
Salome and Elizabeth are her cousins. If every stated relationship were
biological, each named woman and both named sons would receive mtDNA along
the same maternal tree. The text does not establish further children of
Salome, Elizabeth, or Mary; the midwife Salome should not automatically be
identified with Mary Salome in another legend.

## 3. Medieval Holy Kinship

The [*Golden Legend*, chapter 131](https://www.christianiconography.info/goldenLegend/maryNativity.htm)
and this [Holy Kinship overview](https://college.holycross.edu/projects/kempe/devotion/holy_kin/holykin.html)
describe Anne and Hismeria as sisters. The source does not name their
mother; **[shared mother]** is a conditional inference if they were maternal
or full sisters, not a person named in this passage.

```text
[shared mother, unnamed and conditional]
├── Anne
│   ├── Mary, mother of Jesus ──→ Jesus
│   ├── Mary Cleophas ──→ James the Less, Joseph, Simon, Jude
│   └── Mary Salome   ──→ James the Greater, John the Evangelist
└── Hismeria ──→ Elizabeth ──→ John the Baptist
```

Anne's three daughters are maternal half-sisters in this legend, so their
children would ordinarily receive Anne's maternal mtDNA **if** the
relationships were biological. John the Evangelist here is not John the
Baptist. The legend does not establish daughters of the two Marys or trace
their maternal descendants further.

The legend also mentions a branch through **Eliud → Eminen → Servatius**.
The [Caxton translation](https://www.christianiconography.info/goldenLegend/maryNativity.htm)
calls Eliud one of Hismeria's “two daughters,” whereas a
[medieval painting's interpretation](https://www.christianiconography.info/Wikimedia%20Commons/holyKinship.html)
identifies Eliud as her son and Eminen as his son. Because these witnesses
are inconsistent, whether a first link from Eliud continues Hismeria's
mtDNA needs a check of the underlying text and variant traditions. In the
painting's male-line interpretation, Eliud's children would not ordinarily
inherit his maternal mtDNA. Neither version proves a line to the fourth-century
bishop or to modern families.

## 4. A reported Emerentia version

The compiler's footnote in [Emmerich, chapter 1, note 16](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/emmerich/lifemary.v.html)
reports an alternative traditional tree. Its specific original source and
earliest form still need checking:

```text
Emerentia
├── Anne     ──→ Mary, mother of Jesus ──→ Jesus
└── Ismeria  ──→ Elizabeth ──→ John the Baptist
```

If biological, both branches would ordinarily carry Emerentia's maternal
mtDNA. This short reported tree does not itself establish that Emerentia
was the mother of the three sisters in the Byzantine tree.

## 5. Emmerich's visionary tree, with more branches

The nineteenth-century account recorded by Clemens Brentano gives a
different set of relationships. The named branches below come from
[chapter 1](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/emmerich/lifemary.v.html); its
visionary account is much later than the people depicted and is not
independent verification of their parentage.

```text
Emorun / Moruni
├── Ismeria
│   ├── Sobe
│   │   ├── Mary Salome ──→ James and John, sons of Zebedee
│   │   ├── unnamed daughter ──→ three disciples (names not given here)
│   │   └── Eliud (son)
│   ├── Anne
│   │   ├── elder daughter Mary
│   │   └── Mary, mother of Jesus ──→ Jesus
│   ├── Maraha
│   │   ├── unnamed daughter
│   │   └── Arastaria and Cocharia (sons)
│   └── another sister, unnamed
├── Emerentia
│   ├── Elizabeth ──→ John the Baptist
│   ├── Enue (daughter)
│   └── Rhoda ──→ Mara
└── Enue (Emorun's daughter; distinct from Emerentia's daughter Enue)
```

Here **Ismeria** is Anne's mother, and **Emerentia** is Ismeria's sister,
not Anne's mother. Sobe, Maraha, and the unnamed sister are Anne's sisters;
their daughters are potential maternal-line branches *within this account*.
The account also mentions additional unnamed siblings. It says Enue,
Emorun's daughter, had a descendant associated with Jesus, without supplying
an uninterrupted maternal path to that person in the passage cited.

The two named women **Rhoda and Mara**, and the younger **Enue**, belong to
Emerentia's branch in this version. All the arrows shown would imply shared
maternal inheritance if the claims were biologically accurate. The sons
listed could carry the line but would not ordinarily pass it to their
children. Names repeated in another tree cannot be assumed to identify the
same person or the same family position.

## 6. What could take the line farther?

| Lead | What would help | Current limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anna's parentage | Identify the earliest accessible primary texts for each proposed mother and compare exact wording. | Different traditions name different women; none is a verified first-century maternal record. |
| Aunts and cousins | Check each source's parent–child wording, including unnamed daughters and variants, before adding descendants. | A relationship such as “relative” or “sister” does not always specify biological maternal kinship. |
| Possible DNA comparison | Verified remains or a reliable reference sample, provenance, independent analysis, and documented maternal links would be needed. | No verified sample for Jesus or Mary is identified here. A shared haplogroup alone cannot identify a named ancestor. |
| More distant origins | Compare responsibly dated population and ancient-DNA research separately from the family charts. | No evidence currently links a specific ancient population, goddess, or haplogroup to this named family. |

Matthew and Luke give longer genealogies, but [Matthew 1 and Luke 3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%201%3A1-16%3BLuke%203%3A23-38&version=NRSVUE)
list ancestry through Joseph; Luke does not explicitly identify his list
as Mary's. They are not an uninterrupted mother-to-mother mtDNA path.
