# Sekhmet, Rebis, and the Rose

The conversation opens by asking about "Skemet" and develops a fictional story
about an awakening heir, the Rebis, a rose line, and repeated cycles. These
are distinct layers to compare, not a single documented ancient tradition.

## Three separate starting points

- **Sekhmet:** An ancient Egyptian lioness goddess associated with both
  destructive power and healing. See [Britannica's overview](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Sekhmet).
- **Rebis:** An image of united opposites in European alchemy. This
  [15th-century manuscript miniature](https://openn.library.upenn.edu/Data/0023/html/lewis_e_m_028_026-027.html)
  depicts a crowned figure described by its library as a Rebis or androgyne.
  Its original accompanying text is unknown.
- **Rose line:** In the shared conversation this is a creative motif of a
  hidden lineage and an awakening heir. The response connects the rose to
  secrecy and a preserved sacred maternal line; that broad historical claim
  requires specific sources and cannot establish an actual genealogy.

## Compare without flattening

Possible story themes include protection and destruction, hidden identity,
the union of opposites, and rediscovering agency. A theme shared by two works
does not show that they describe the same being, or that one borrowed from
the other.

See the [light, darkness, and Rebis investigation](../light-dark-rebis/pairings-and-maternal-source.md)
for a comparison of proposed divine pairs and the maternal-thread idea.

For each comparison record the artifact or text, culture, date, what it
actually depicts, and what the creative story adds. Avoid equating a modern
character or a living person's family history with an ancient deity based on
a name, visual resemblance, or symbolic pattern.
