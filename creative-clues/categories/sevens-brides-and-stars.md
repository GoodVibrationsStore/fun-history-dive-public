# Seven Brides, Stars, Lamps, and Thunder

**Question:** Which connections in the chat come from named texts, and which
are creative overlays?

## A trail that really connects

The 1954 film *Seven Brides for Seven Brothers* drew on Stephen Vincent
Benét's story *The Sobbin' Women*, which reworks the Roman tradition of the
abduction of the Sabine women as told by Plutarch. See
[Turner Classic Movies on the film's source](https://www.tcm.com/articles/191150/the-big-idea-seven-brides-for-seven-brothers).
The stories involve abduction; the film's comic treatment should not be
mistaken for evidence of consent.

[Judges 21:19–23](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Judges%2021%3A19-23&version=NRSVUE)
also depicts men carrying off women at Shiloh. That is a thematic comparison,
not evidence it inspired the film or that either account refers to the
Pleiades. Nor does the shared number establish that the film's brides are
the seven star-sisters.

## Nearby clues, different meanings

| Clue | Text or source | What it actually describes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seven sisters | [Greek sources on Alcyone](https://www.theoi.com/Nymphe/NympheAlkyone.html) | The Pleiades are seven daughters of Atlas and Pleione in Greek myth. See [the Pleiades note](./pleiades-and-mythic-overlays.md) for variable lists. |
| Pleiades and Orion | [Job 38:31–33 and Amos 5:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Job%2038%3A31-33%3BAmos%205%3A8&version=NRSVUE) | English translations identify Hebrew star names with these sky objects. The [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/12206-pleiades) discusses the traditional identification of Kimah. The passages do not narrate the Greek sisters' family story. |
| Seven lamps | [Exodus 25:31–37](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2025%3A31-37&version=NRSVUE) | A lampstand has a central shaft, six branches, and seven lamps. It does not identify a mother and six daughters. |
| Seven stars and lampstands | [Revelation 1:20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation%201%3A20&version=NRSVUE) | The text interprets the stars as angels of seven churches and the lampstands as those churches; it does not identify them as the Pleiades. |
| Bride and bridegroom | [John 3:25–30](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%203%3A25-30&version=NRSVUE) | The Baptist is described as the bridegroom's friend in an image of joy and relative roles. The passage does not identify seven brides or a historical marriage. |
| Sons of Thunder | [Mark 3:17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%203%3A17&version=NRSVUE) | James and John, sons of Zebedee, receive this name. This John is distinct from John the Baptist. |
| Seven thunders | [Revelation 10:3–4](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation%2010%3A3-4&version=NRSVUE) | Seven thunders sound, and their words are sealed. The passage does not identify them with the two sons of Zebedee or seven people. |
| Seven seals | [Revelation 6:1–8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation%206%3A1-8&version=NRSVUE) | The white horse appears with the **first** seal; the fourth brings a pale green horse and Death. The chat reversed that detail. |

The actual Pleiades are a star cluster in Taurus, not the constellation
Orion. The [Orionid meteors](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/meteors-meteorites/orionids/)
come from debris of Halley's Comet; their apparent radiant is near Orion, not
their physical source. Celestial imagery can inspire comparisons without
establishing a family connection or forecasting personal events.
