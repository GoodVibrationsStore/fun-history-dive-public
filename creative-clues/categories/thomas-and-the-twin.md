# Thomas and the Twin Question

The chat asks whether Jesus had a twin, and then specifically asks about a
twin **sister**. Its answer blends a real ancient Thomas tradition, modern
interpretations, and some unsupported details.

## What the sources say

| Source | Evidence | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [John 11:16 and 20:24](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2011%3A16%3BJohn%2020%3A24&version=NRSVUE) | The disciple Thomas is called Didymus, “the Twin.” | John does not identify whose twin he is. |
| [Mark 6:3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%206%3A3&version=NRSVUE) | Names Jesus's brothers James, Joses, Judas, and Simon and mentions unnamed sisters. | It does not identify Thomas as that Judas, name a sister, or say Jesus had a twin. Different Christian traditions interpret the brothers and sisters differently. |
| [Gospel of Thomas](https://www.earlychristianwritings.com/text/thomas-scholars.html) | Its opening attributes the recorded sayings to Didymos Judas Thomas. | This label alone does not say he is Jesus's biological twin or prove who wrote the work. |
| [Book of Thomas the Contender](https://www.luminescence-llc.net/thomas-the-contender) | The Savior addresses Thomas as brother and refers to him as his twin and true friend. The surviving Coptic manuscript is in Nag Hammadi Codex II. | The text does not narrate Mary giving birth to twins; a spiritual or literary reading is possible. Its manuscript date and composition date are different questions. |
| [Acts of Thomas, opening episode](https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/apocryphal_acts_07_judas_thomas.htm) | In a Syriac version, the bridegroom sees Jesus in Judas's likeness; Jesus says he is Judas's brother. | This is an ancient narrative of likeness and kinship, not independent documentation of a first-century birth. Its versions and interpretations should be compared. |

Thomas derives from an Aramaic word for twin, and Didymus is Greek for twin.
The [*Gorgias Encyclopedic Dictionary of the Syriac Heritage*](https://gedsh.bethmardutho.org/Thomas-Acts-of)
describes the *Acts of Thomas* as probably third-century, originally in
Syriac, with an early Greek version. It notes that treating the whole work
as simply “Gnostic” is contested. This is the same broader work that
preserves the [Hymn of the Pearl](./pearl-and-labyrinth.md), though the hymn's
relationship to the work is debated.

The twin language is a genuine lead worth reading, not something to dismiss.
But the chat's assertion of an **established identical biological twin** goes
beyond these sources. The Gospel of Thomas's opening is not itself a claim of
identical appearance; the wedding scene explicitly uses likeness and
brotherhood, with interpretation still open.

## The sister question

Mark mentions sisters without names or birth order. The sources checked here
do not establish that one was Jesus's twin. The chat's claim that Thomas had
a twin sister named Lysia or Lydia needs a precise source; do not treat that
relationship as verified. Mythic male-female twins and fictional doubles are
separate creative comparisons.

### A possible source of the names

The [*History of Joseph the Carpenter*, chapter 2](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0805.htm)
names Joseph's daughters from an earlier marriage **Assia and Lydia** in
that English translation. A [scholarly overview](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/history-of-joseph-the-carpenter/)
uses **Lysia and Lydia** in its summary. The work places them in Joseph's
family before Mary; neither of those descriptions makes either woman a
twin of Thomas or Jesus. The translation and overview use different forms
for one name, so checking the relevant manuscript traditions is a further
step—not grounds to assume their precise relationship. The narrative is a
later apocryphal account, not a contemporaneous birth record.

If these women were Joseph's daughters from an earlier marriage as this
story proposes, their maternal mtDNA would ordinarily come from their own
mother, not from Mary simply because she joined their household.

**Next step:** Compare the Syriac and Greek versions of the *Acts*, and find
the manuscript forms of the sisters' names and any source actually claiming
the proposed twin relationship.
