# Maternal Line: Emerentia, Emorun, and the Rose

## The clue

The shared chat does name **Emerentia** as the mother of Anne and Ismeria,
and therefore as great-grandmother of both Jesus and John the Baptist. That
is probably the name being remembered. Its later claim that Emerentia was
also the mother of Mary, Sobe, and Anne merges different family-tree
versions. The names and relationships need to be checked one source at a
time.

The following are accounts of traditions and visions, not verified genetic
pedigrees. An arrow means "mother of" in the particular account.

## 1. Earliest layers: where the record stops

- [Luke 1:5, 36, 39–45](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%201%3A5-7%2C36%2C39-45&version=NRSVUE)
  names Mary as Jesus's mother and Elizabeth as John the Baptist's mother,
  and calls Elizabeth Mary's relative. It does not specify their exact
  relationship or show a shared maternal-line ancestor. Elizabeth's descent
  from Aaron does not specify an all-female line.
- The second-century [Protevangelium of James, chapters 1–5](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0847.htm)
  names Anna and Joachim as Mary's parents, but does not name Anna's mother.
  Its genealogy is a later tradition, not independent proof of those
  relationships; see the [NASSCAL overview](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/protevangelium-of-james/).

Thus Mary is the earliest named maternal ancestor of Jesus in Luke, while
Anna is the next named candidate in this second-century tradition. Neither
source provides an uninterrupted maternal tree connecting Jesus and John.

## 2. Emerentia as the shared great-grandmother

A note by the compiler in [Anne Catherine Emmerich's *Life of the Blessed
Virgin Mary*, chapter 1, footnote 16](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/emmerich/lifemary.v.html)
describes a tradition in which Emerentia, wife of Stolanus, is the mother of
Anne and Ismeria, and Ismeria is Elizabeth's mother:

```text
Emerentia
├── Anne → Mary mother of Jesus → Jesus
└── Ismeria → Elizabeth → John the Baptist
```

In that reported version, Emerentia would be the **great-grandmother of
both**, and Mary and Elizabeth would be first cousins. The compiler does not
give the specific original source for this particular tree in that note, so
its earliest attestation remains to be checked. The medieval [Holy Kinship
account at Holy Cross](https://college.holycross.edu/projects/kempe/devotion/holy_kin/holykin.html)
independently describes Anne and Hismeria as sisters, with Elizabeth as
Hismeria's daughter, but does not name their mother.

In the Holy Kinship version, Anne also has three daughters named Mary;
Mary Salome is the mother of James and John, the sons of Zebedee. This is
not the only Salome tradition, and John son of Zebedee is **not** John the
Baptist. See [Anne, Elizabeth, Salome, and the family-tree puzzle](./anne-elizabeth-salome.md).

Some late-medieval texts call Anne's mother Emerentia and give her a
prophetic root-and-tree vision. An artwork from approximately 1515–1530
shows Emerentia, Anne, Mary, and Jesus together. See [Christian Iconography](https://www.christianiconography.info/emerentia.html)
and its [artwork description](https://www.christianiconography.info/december2001/emerantia.html).
This establishes a later devotional tradition, not a first-century record.

## 3. Mary wife of Matthan: the three-sister version

In a [secondary source quoting a later Byzantine family-tree tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobe_(sister_of_Saint_Anne)),
the mother of the three sisters is named **Mary**, wife of Matthan:

```text
Mary wife of Matthan
├── elder Mary → Salome the midwife
├── Sobe → Elizabeth → John the Baptist
└── Anne → Mary mother of Jesus → Jesus
```

If these relationships were biological and correct, Mary wife of Matthan
would be a shared maternal great-grandmother of Jesus and John. She is
distinct within this tree from both her daughter Mary and her granddaughter
Mary. The quotation gives the order of the three sisters as shown. Its
original Greek and transmission need further checking; it does not name
their mother Emerentia. The Salome it names as a midwife is not automatically
the mother of Zebedee's sons.

## 4. Emorun and the rose: Emmerich's different visionary tree

The nineteenth-century visionary account recorded by Clemens Brentano gives
an older named maternal ancestor, **Moruni or Emorun**, and a different set
of relationships. In [chapter 1](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/emmerich/lifemary.v.html),
the visionary says Emorun and Stolanus had daughters Ismeria, Emerentia,
and a younger daughter whose name she thinks was Enue:

```text
Emorun (Moruni)
├── Ismeria
│   ├── Sobe
│   ├── Anne → Mary mother of Jesus → Jesus
│   └── Maraha
├── Emerentia → Elizabeth → John the Baptist
└── Enue
```

Here Emorun would be Jesus's **great-great-grandmother** and John's
**great-grandmother**. Ismeria and Emerentia are sisters, not alternate
names for one person; Emerentia is Anne's aunt, not her mother. Sobe is
Anne's older sister, but in this version she is **not** Elizabeth's mother.
The compiler suggests Emorun might be a form of Emerentia but acknowledges
the conflicting genealogies; that suggestion does not make the figures
interchangeable within this account.

The same narrative describes a prophet seeing a **rose tree with three
branches and three roses** growing from beneath Emorun's heart. It is an
intriguing rose-line motif in the text, not evidence that the vision records
an ancient family or biological inheritance.

### The vision, step by step

In the [same chapter](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/emmerich/lifemary.v.html),
Emorun seeks marriage advice from Archos, described as an Essene prophet on
Mount Horeb. He sees the rose tree; the second rose bears a mark that the
visionary thinks is the letter M. He advises her to marry her sixth suitor,
Stolanus. The account later says their daughter Ismeria gives birth to Anne,
who bears a sign, after a separate revelation to Ismeria. Archos is the
prophet in the story, not Emorun's husband.

This gives us two different **tree** motifs to compare: Emorun's three roses
in the nineteenth-century visionary account, and the earlier Emerentia
root-and-trees story discussed above. Their similar imagery does not prove
the two names designate the same historical person or establish that one
vision was hidden to protect descendants.

## 5. What the mtDNA diagrams mean—and do not mean

Mitochondrial DNA is ordinarily inherited from the mother. Sons and
daughters can receive it, but sons do not ordinarily pass it to their
children. In each *hypothetical* tree above, the female links would allow
both Jesus and John to inherit mtDNA from the stated shared maternal
ancestor; maternal-line descendants could have very similar sequences,
subject to mutations and heteroplasmy. A branch through a father to his
child does not ordinarily continue that mtDNA line.

No verified reference DNA sample or documented chain here establishes
Jesus's or John's mtDNA. A shared haplogroup is not unique to a named
ancestor and cannot prove that any of these women existed, that a spiritual
role was inherited, or that a title was stolen. See [Genetics and lineage
claims](./genetics-and-lineage-claims.md).

## 6. The Barbelo/reincarnation lead

The precise story that a grandmother was believed to be Barbelo reincarnated
but was deliberately unnamed to protect her descendants has **not been
found in the passages reviewed**.

One separate ancient comparison is **Helena**, whom [Irenaeus, *Against
Heresies* 1.23.2–3](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103123.htm), reports
that Simon Magus described as Ennoia, his first thought, passing through
successive female bodies. This is a hostile second-century account of
another group's beliefs, not independent verification. The [*Apocryphon of
John*](https://earlychristianwritings.com/text/apocryphonjohn.html) calls
Barbelo the first thought, making a motif comparison possible. Neither
passage establishes that Helena was Barbelo, an ancestor of Jesus or John,
or part of an mtDNA lineage, nor does the Irenaeus passage give the
lineage-protection explanation.

The chat also contains a creative Sophia/Barbelo and Anne/Mary overlay.
That may be part of the remembered combination, but it is not evidence that
an ancient source made the same identification. The chat's claim of a
suppressed cult of Emerentia's conception likewise needs a specific
historical source; none was supplied in the passage reviewed.

### A closer match for the hidden-light motif

In Wisse's [translation of the *Apocryphon of John*](https://earlychristianwritings.com/text/apocryphonjohn.html),
the mother asks to recover her power, and the Mother-Father sends five
lights who advise Yaldabaoth to breathe into Adam. His mother's power then
passes into Adam. The benevolent Mother-Father also sends a helper, luminous
Epinoia, who is hidden in Adam so the rulers will not know her.

That is a real ancient text containing **maternal power, a helper, and
concealment from rulers**. It does not say Emorun was Barbelo, identify
Adam's hidden helper with Jesus's grandmother, or explain concealment as
protection of a named family or mtDNA line. It also contradicts the chat's
blanket claim that Sophia received no help. The *Apocryphon* is a distinct
work from the nineteenth-century rose vision; the motifs can be compared
without treating them as one event.

## Open leads

- Find the earliest primary source for the Emerentia–Ismeria–Anne tree
  quoted by Brentano, and check its exact wording.
- Check the Greek text behind the Matthan and Mary three-sister version.
- Continue reviewing the shared chat for the specific incarnation and
  lineage-protection story without treating all responses as read.
