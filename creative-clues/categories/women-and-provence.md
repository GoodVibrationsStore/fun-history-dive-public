# The Women and the Voyage to Provence

**Question:** Which connections are in early Christian texts, and which belong
to later traditions?

## Source-by-source observations

| Source | What it says | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Luke 8:1–3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%208%3A1-3&version=NRSVUE) | Names Mary Magdalene, Joanna, Susanna, and other women who supported the group from their resources. | Martha is not named in this list. |
| [John 11:1–5, 20–27](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=John%2011%3A1-27&version=NRSVUE) | Describes Martha, Mary, and Lazarus as siblings at Bethany; Martha makes a confession about Jesus. | The text does not identify this Mary as Mary Magdalene or give the siblings' birth order. |
| [Mark 16:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%2016%3A1&version=NRSVUE) | Names Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James, and Salome at the tomb. | It does not name Sarah or describe a voyage to France. |
| [The Golden Legend, life of Martha](https://www.christianiconography.info/goldenLegend/martha.htm) | A medieval saint's life tells of Martha, Lazarus, Mary, Maximin, and others reaching Marseilles by boat; it also tells the Tarasque story. | A 13th-century legend is not an eyewitness account of a first-century voyage. Sarah and the two Marys are not named among the passengers in this particular chapter. |

The [Golden Legend's Magdalene chapter](https://www.christianiconography.info/goldenLegend/magdalene.htm)
identifies Magdalene with Martha's sister and with the unnamed sinner. That
is a medieval conflation; the Gospel passages do not explicitly make those
identifications. Different versions of the Provence tradition name different
travelers and describe Sarah differently. Her place in local and Romani
devotion deserves investigation on its own terms, not an assumed genealogy.

## A textual puzzle worth checking

[Elizabeth Schrader's study](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0017816016000213)
examines variation around Martha in early manuscripts of John and proposes that
she may not have appeared in an earlier form of that Gospel. This is a
scholarly hypothesis, not proof of the original text, an identified motive, or
a demonstrated bloodline. The study's accessible abstract was checked; the
full article and scholarly responses still need review.

## Peter's mother

The New Testament does not name Peter's mother. His unnamed mother-in-law
appears in [Mark 1:29–31](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%201%3A29-31&version=NRSVUE).
The shared chat's claim that Peter's mother was called Joanna needs a specific
dated source; do not treat it as established. Its attribution of the onion
parable in *The Brothers Karamazov* to Peter's mother also needs checking:
the version told by Grushenka features an unnamed woman.
