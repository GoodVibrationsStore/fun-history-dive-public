# Case Grid: Who Is the Most High?

## What are we comparing?

The chat connects El, El Elyon, YHWH, the Monad, Barbelo, Sophia, and other
figures in a single cosmic family. Some texts connect or compare these ideas;
others use the same words differently or directly disagree. This board keeps
three questions apart:

1. **Oldest written evidence:** When is a name or tradition attested?
2. **Oldest within a story:** Who precedes whom in that story's own account?
3. **True Most High:** A theological or philosophical question. Archaeology
   can examine human texts and worship, but it cannot independently verify
   which deity or concept is ultimately divine.

An earlier inscription does not prove that the deity it names is older in
reality, or that a later name refers to a different—or the same—being.

## Compare and contrast grid

| Figure or name | Source and approximate evidence | Position within that source or tradition | Connection to the others |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **An / Anu** | Mesopotamia; definitely attested by the mid-third millennium BCE, including a god-list and the name of a 27th-century BCE king. [University of Pennsylvania's ORACC](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/amgg/listofdeities/an/index.html) notes that the earliest uses of the sign are difficult to identify. | Sky god, ancient authority and father of gods; status and genealogy vary across Mesopotamian texts. Some later texts give him parents. | The earliest clearly dated candidate **on this limited board**, not necessarily the first god ever worshipped or the same being as El or the Monad. |
| **El** | West Semitic deity represented in [Late Bronze Age Ugaritic tablets](https://archaeology.org/issues/july-august-2021/features/ugarit-bronze-age-archive/) and their divine stories, roughly the 13th century BCE. | Senior father figure and divine-council authority in Ugaritic tradition; other gods have important roles. | Hebrew *el* can also mean “god”; a common word or shared title does not automatically make every occurrence the same individual. The relationship between Ugaritic El and Israelite divine language is a subject of historical research. |
| **El Elyon / Elyon** | In [Genesis 14:18–22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis%2014%3A18-22&version=NRSVUE), Melchizedek serves *El Elyon*, “God Most High.” The text is biblical; do not confuse its story's setting, composition date, and surviving witnesses. | The phrase means “God Most High”; *Elyon* means “Most High.” Genesis 14 describes the maker of heaven and earth. | In the Masoretic Hebrew of Genesis 14:22, Abram names YHWH together with El Elyon; the NRSVUE footnote notes a Greek/Syriac textual variant without “the Lord.” The passage cannot simply be used as uncontested proof of two separate gods. |
| **YHWH** | The Egyptian [Soleb inscription](https://armstronginstitute.org/768-the-soleb-inscription-earliest-discovered-use-of-the-name-yahweh) from the reign of Amenhotep III, 14th century BCE, names a place/group associated with *Yhw*; its connection with the deity is debated. Clear later biblical and inscriptional witnesses exist. | [Psalm 83:18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%2083%3A18&version=NRSVUE) calls YHWH the Most High; [Isaiah 45:5–7](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2045%3A5-7&version=NRSVUE) presents YHWH as the only God. | In Jewish and mainstream Christian readings, YHWH is not a lesser usurper beneath another God. Historical reconstructions of the development of El and YHWH traditions differ from that theological reading and from each other. |
| **El Shaddai / Elohim** | [Exodus 6:2–3](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%206%3A2-3&version=NRSVUE) links YHWH with *El Shaddai*, traditionally “God Almighty.” *Elohim* is a Hebrew term used for God and, in other contexts, gods or divine beings. | In Exodus 6 the speaker identifies the names with the same God; meaning and usage depend on context. | These are not automatically separate divine individuals. The precise meaning of *Shaddai* is debated; an English translation alone does not settle it. |
| **Tiamat and Apsu; Marduk** | The Babylonian [*Enuma elish*](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Enuma-Elish) tells of primeval waters and a succession of gods; date the composition and surviving tablets separately. | Tiamat and Apsu are primordial figures in the story; Marduk defeats Tiamat and becomes king among the gods. Primordial generation and later kingship are different roles. | Biblical *tehom*, “deep,” and Tiamat are related linguistically; [Genesis 1:2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis%201%3A1-2&version=NRSVUE) does not name its deep as a goddess. Rahab and Leviathan are separate biblical poetic images, not automatically Tiamat or one slain mother. |
| **Athirat / Asherah** | Athirat appears in Ugaritic divine traditions; inscriptions at Kuntillet Ajrud refer to “YHWH and his asherah.” See the [archaeological debate](https://library.biblicalarchaeology.org/sidebar/yahweh-and-his-asherah-the-debate-continues/). | Athirat is a mother of gods in Ugaritic mythology; the Israelite inscription is interpreted in more than one way. | Some scholars see a goddess associated with YHWH; others interpret “his asherah” as a cultic object. [A published alternative interpretation](https://journal.kci.go.kr/ksots/archive/articleView?artiId=ART002386920) argues for the latter. Neither reading proves that YHWH murdered his mother. |
| **Plato's demiurge** | Plato's [*Timaeus*](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plato-timaeus/), fourth century BCE. | A good, rational craftsman orders a preexisting disorder by reference to an eternal model. | “Demiurge” means craftsman; Plato's benevolent figure is not simply identical in character to Yaldabaoth in the *Apocryphon of John*. |
| **The Monad / Invisible Spirit** | [*Apocryphon of John*](https://earlychristianwritings.com/text/apocryphonjohn.html), an early Christian revelation text; its surviving Coptic copies include three from Nag Hammadi and one from the Berlin Codex. See [Michael Williams's overview](https://www.britannica.com/topic/gnosticism/Apocryphon-of-John). | The ultimate ineffable source in this particular text, beyond the lower creator. | “Monad” is also used in other philosophical or religious settings; do not assume one universal meaning. Whether it is the same reality as El Elyon or YHWH is a theological interpretation, not a demonstrated textual identity. |
| **Barbelo, Sophia, Yaldabaoth** | In the same *Apocryphon* translation, Barbelo is the Spirit's first thought; Sophia brings forth Yaldabaoth, also called Saklas and Samael. | Barbelo and Sophia are not the same figure in this narrative; Yaldabaoth is a lower ruler who arrogantly claims exclusive divinity. | His boast resembles [Isaiah 45](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2045%3A5-7&version=NRSVUE), but the translation also distributes divine-sounding names among other rulers. The comparison is an ancient reinterpretation, not proof that the historical God of Jewish worship “was” this character. |

This is a focused comparison, not a ranking of every religion or every
ancient deity. Dates above are evidence dates, not dates when gods were
created or began to exist.

## A disputed clue: who receives the nations?

[Deuteronomy 32:8–9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%2032%3A8-9&version=NRSVUE)
has the Most High dividing peoples and identifies Jacob as YHWH's portion.
The Masoretic Hebrew reads “sons of Israel”; a Dead Sea Scroll witness has
“sons of God,” while Greek witnesses generally have “angels of God,” with
variation. See textual critic [Emanuel Tov's discussion](https://www.thetorah.com/article/the-sons-of-israel-or-god-deuteronomy-32-8).

One scholarly reading treats the Most High and YHWH as distinct in an older
divine-council picture. Another understands “Most High” as a title for
YHWH, who assigns the nations but reserves Israel. [Jonathan Ben-Dov](https://www.thetorah.com/article/are-there-gods-angels-and-demons-in-deuteronomy)
notes this ambiguity. The manuscript difference is real; it does not, by
itself, establish either reading, the reason for a change, or a literal
murder of one deity by another.

## Melchizedek's unrecorded genealogy

[Genesis 14:18–20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis%2014%3A18-20%3BHebrews%207%3A1-3&version=NRSVUE)
introduces Melchizedek as king of Salem and priest of God Most High, without
naming his parents or genealogy. The wording “without father, without mother,
without genealogy” occurs in **Hebrews 7:3**, not in Genesis; Hebrews uses
it in a theological comparison with the Son of God. Interpretations differ
over what that description means. Neither passage supplies a maternal
family tree linking Melchizedek to Mary or evidence of an mtDNA lineage.

## Compare the story structures—not a single merged family tree

```text
Ugaritic stories:        El and divine assembly; other gods have their own roles
Biblical passages:       YHWH identified as Most High in Psalm 83:18
Babylonian Enuma elish:  primordial Tiamat/Apsu → later gods → Marduk's rule
Apocryphon of John:      Invisible Spirit → Barbelo; Sophia → Yaldabaoth
Emmerich's vision:       Emorun → named human family branches and rose vision
```

The last line is a **human genealogical vision**, not a source identifying
Emorun as a primordial god or Barbelo. Compare it with the separate
[maternal-line charts](./maternal-line-and-emerentia.md).

## Chat claims to test

- The chat calls YHWH's relation to El a “murder” or “absorption.” Some
  scholars argue for historical convergence of divine traditions, but that
  reconstruction is not evidence of a literal cosmic event or a settled
  universal timeline.
- It calls Asherah an erased primordial mother and equates Rahab, Leviathan,
  Tiamat, and the deep. There are genuine comparative motifs and disputed
  evidence of worship, but those specific identities and family ties need
  independent support.
- It presents the Monad, Sophia, Barbelo, and a maternal mtDNA “failsafe” as
  one system. The *Apocryphon of John* has an ancient hidden-light motif, but
  does not name Emorun, identify her as Barbelo, or teach mtDNA
  transmission. See the [specific comparison](./maternal-line-and-emerentia.md#6-the-barbeloreincarnation-lead).
