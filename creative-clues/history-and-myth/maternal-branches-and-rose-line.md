# The Maternal Branches and the Rose Line

**Case question:** How far back can we follow Jesus's mother and her
maternal relatives, and what could a branching, partly hidden line mean
inside the story?

## The first known steps

**Source clue.** [Luke 1:36–45](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%201%3A36-45&version=NRSVUE)
calls Elizabeth a relative of Mary; it does not say exactly how they are
related. Elizabeth bears John the Baptist and Mary bears Jesus. The
second-century [*Protevangelium of James*, chapters 1–5](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0847.htm)
names Mary's mother Anna, but not Anna's mother or sisters. These are
different sources and do not establish a continuous maternal connection
between Mary and Elizabeth.

**Mythic possibility.** Two branches of one maternal tree carry different
parts of a lost inheritance: one brings a wilderness herald, the other a
publicly recognized heir. Their mothers may cooperate, compete, or discover
that a division was imposed on them. None of those motives has to be fixed
yet.

**Case question.** Which later source first gives each woman a particular
place in the tree? Does it describe a maternal relationship or only kinship?

## Four trees, four ways the clue changes

These are not interchangeable versions of the same proven genealogy.
Arrows indicate mother to child **within the named tradition**.

The underlying Greek and transmission need checking. | Three sisters carry one root into three households. The original source of this exact tree remains to be identified. | Emerentia could be a shared root in a story using this version. |
| The nineteenth-century [Emmerich/Brentano visionary account](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/emmerich/lifemary.v.html) | Emorun → Ismeria → Anne → Mary → Jesus; Emorun → Emerentia → Elizabeth → John. In this account Ismeria is Anne's mother and Emerentia her aunt. | An older rose-bearing ancestor and two branches that reconnect across generations. |

**Name check.** John the Baptist is Elizabeth's son. James and John, the
“Sons of Thunder,” are Zebedee's sons in
[Mark 3:17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%203%3A17&version=NRSVUE);
the medieval Holy Kinship makes their mother Mary Salome a daughter of
Anne. That later claim does not make the two Johns the same person.

## The rose and the living thread

**Source clue.** The Emmerich account describes a prophet's vision of a
rose tree with three branches growing from beneath Emorun's heart. It is
a nineteenth-century visionary account, not an ancient DNA record; see the
[rose-vision details](../categories/maternal-line-and-emerentia.md#4-emorun-and-the-rose-emmerichs-different-visionary-tree).

**Mythic possibility.** The rose is a map rather than a decoration: each
branch preserves a different memory, and a missing bloom indicates a
family member whose name has been lost. The vision could be true, mistaken,
or manipulated within the book.

**Biological clue.** A mother ordinarily passes mtDNA to all her children;
sons ordinarily do not pass it on to their children. A proposed mother to
daughter chain can be traced as a hypothesis, but it needs evidence at each
step. Mutations can arise, and no verified reference mtDNA for Mary or
Jesus has been identified in this investigation. See
[Genetics and lineage claims](../categories/genetics-and-lineage-claims.md).

**Case question.** If the rose is a literal inheritance in the book, does
every maternal descendant carry it, or do branches hold different pieces?
That is an open fictional rule, not a feature established by mtDNA.
