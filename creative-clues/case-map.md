# Case Map: Lineage, Teachings, and the Rose

## The investigation

What can sources tell us about Jesus's family, the women around him, and how
traditions about them developed? Where did teachings and stories change, and
what evidence exists for the causes of those changes?

The shared conversation begins with a question about Sekhmet and includes a
creative hidden-heir narrative, the rose line, maternal-family theories,
ancient religious texts, songwriting, and pop-culture comparisons. The loaded
page contains hundreds of exchanges; its topics have been mapped and selected
passages reviewed, but not every response has been read in full.

Recent additional sections reviewed include the Pearl and labyrinth, Norse
family and oaths, and selected discussions of Watchers, El, Baal, Jubilees,
4 Ezra, Jacob's household, tribal imagery, Sophia and Barbelo, the Thomas
twin tradition, and Passover and Grail imagery. Many responses, including
non-song exchanges, still remain to be read; the notes are a working
investigation rather than a complete transcript.

The latest focused review adds Emorun's rose vision, a hidden-light passage,
and the chat's claims about El Elyon, YHWH, Sophia, and the Monad. These
are selected passages, not a full review of the conversation.

A further selected review covers the five-figure Sophia sequence, the
planet-and-weekday chart, Cronus and Rhea, and women in the Gospel stories.
The [Norea comparison](./traditions-and-eras/early-christian/sophia-eleleth-and-hidden-line.md)
records a protected spiritual root and future seed; the
[planet comparison](./traditions-and-eras/greek-olympian/planets-weekdays-and-archons.md)
checks the actual seven rulers against the chat's chart.

A selected passage about ten resets and recurring roles is compared with
flood retellings, protected-child motifs, and different systems of world
ages in [Repeating stories and resets](./categories/repeating-stories-and-resets.md).
This remains a focused review, not a reading of every response.

Another selected review covers the chat's Sophia–Christ inversion and its
Hindu and Buddhist questions. The
[Sophia note](./traditions-and-eras/early-christian/sophia-eleleth-and-hidden-line.md)
now separates heavenly Adamas, earthly Adam, and help for Sophia; the
[Hindu](./traditions-and-eras/hindu/starting-points.md) and

The latest passages compare mothers and divided heirs and explore fictional

## Working rules

- Date the **event described**, the **composition**, and the **surviving
  manuscript** separately when known.
- Do not merge people just because they share a name; identify the source for
  every claimed relationship.
- A later account can show what its community believed without proving what
  happened centuries earlier.
- A missing name, conflicting account, or textual variation is a question, not
  by itself evidence of a deliberate cover-up.
- Record competing explanations and what would change the assessment.

## Current conclusion

There are real differences between sources and documented changes in later
traditions. The material reviewed so far does not establish a deliberate
hijacking of a family line or a single original teaching.
